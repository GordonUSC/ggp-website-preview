The four E's

Built on four pillars. Engineered for impact.

Every program GGP runs maps to one of these — because lifting a community takes more than visibility. It takes pathways, paychecks, and platforms.

01

Education

Scholarships and structured internship programs for LGBTQ+ college students entering gaming and interactive media — putting tuition, mentorship, and industry access where they're needed most.

 02

Expertise

Livestreams and core video content where queer industry leaders go on the record about advancement, craft, and what it actually takes to thrive in games as an underrepresented professional.

 03

Employment

Partnership-based pipelines into major studios — including our long-running Take-Two DEI partnership — placing diverse, qualified talent into the rooms where the next decade of games gets greenlit.

 04

Entrepreneurship

Twitch affiliate and indie creator support — because owning your channel and your IP is how a community stops asking for permission and starts shaping the form.

The story

A community first. A nonprofit second. A movement, always.

GGP began in 2006 as something between a meetup and a movement — queer professionals in gaming finding each other in an industry that was still pretending we weren't there. For more than a decade we ran as community: listservs, late-night meetups at GDC, mentorship that lived in DMs.

In 2018, GGP became a federally-recognized 501(c)(3) — formalizing the work without losing the heart of it. The mission hasn't changed: promote, cultivate, and unite the professional LGBTQ+ community in gaming. The scope just got bigger.

Today GGP is a node in a wider network — Games for Change, GLAAD Gaming, IGDA's LGBTQ+ SIG, and dozens of studio partners — but the work that matters is still the work that started in '06: making sure no queer kid breaking into games has to do it alone.
